The General Counsel

The half of the agency that decides what gets prosecuted. It is not part of the Board, does not report to it, and cannot be overruled by it.

What the office does

  • Decides which charges become complaints. The Act gives the General Counsel final authority over that, and says so in those terms. A charge the General Counsel will not pursue never reaches the Board at all.
  • Prosecutes the case before an administrative law judge, through the regional offices.
  • Supervises the agency's lawyers, other than the personal staff of each Board member and the judges.
  • Hears appeals from dismissals. When a region refuses to issue a complaint, the appeal goes to this office, not to the Board, and must be filed within 14 days.

The office exists because of a complaint about fairness. For twelve years under the Wagner Act the same agency investigated, prosecuted and judged. Management groups said that made it prosecutor and judge at once. Taft-Hartley split the prosecuting half off in 1947 and gave it its own Senate-confirmed head. The rest of that story.
